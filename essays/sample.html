On building software that stays human

Proximity beats the infinite feed. Presence beats the locked screen. This is a starter essay — rewrite every word.

Most social products optimise for time on site. Atlas started from the opposite bet: people you have actually been near should matter more than an algorithm that never leaves your pocket.

That idea sounds small until you try to build it. Bluetooth proximity, a map of posts, no ads — each choice rejects a default the industry treats as inevitable. The technical work is real; the design opinion underneath it is the point.

Lockup asks a similar question in a different room: what if software helped a table of friends stay at the table? Constraint becomes the product. Team friction becomes the curriculum.

I write software because I want the tools I use to leave people more available to each other, not less. If this page still says “sample” when you read it, open essays/_template.html, make a copy, and put your own argument here.
